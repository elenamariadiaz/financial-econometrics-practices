01 · The WTI futures market

Can two nonstationary series move together?

Contract 1 is our proxy for the spot price; Contract 4 expires approximately three months later. Both are rolling futures quotations, not fixed-maturity contracts.

Can prices wander indefinitely while their relationship remains stable?
\[F_{4,t}\approx F_{1,t}e^{(r+u-y)(T_4-T_1)}\]

Under simplified cost-of-carry assumptions, financing, storage and convenience yield determine the relative price. If net carry is stationary, the log calendar spread may be stationary.

02 · From mean reversion to cointegration

A moving equilibrium

\[z_t=y_t-c-\beta x_t\sim I(0),\qquad x_t,y_t\sim I(1)\]

The force of gravity now acts on the relationship rather than on a fixed price. A stationary deviation can coexist with an I(1) common stochastic trend.

High correlation is not enough. Cointegration requires a stationary linear combination.

03 · Testing

From intuition to evidence

Step 1. Integration order

\[\Delta x_t=c+\gamma x_{t-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_i\Delta x_{t-i}+u_t\]

ADF null: unit root (\(\gamma=0\)). Test log levels and first differences, with a constant and AIC-selected lags.

Step 2. Engle–Granger

\[f_{4,t}=c+\beta f_{1,t}+z_t\]

Estimate the long-run relation by OLS. Then test the residual for a unit root using cointegration-specific critical values, not ordinary ADF critical values.

Testing the predetermined unit slope spread is a separate ordinary ADF exercise; it is not a formal restriction test on an estimated Johansen vector.

Step 3. Johansen trace test

\[LR_{trace}(r)=-T\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\log(1-\hat\lambda_i)\]

Starting at rank zero, test the null that the rank is at most r. With two I(1) series, rank one means one cointegrating relation and one common stochastic trend.

04 · Dynamics

Who restores equilibrium?

Cointegration establishes a long-run relationship. The VECM describes the response to disequilibrium.

From VAR to VECM

\[Y_t=A_1Y_{t-1}+\cdots+A_pY_{t-p}+d+u_t\]
↓ subtract \(Y_{t-1}\) and rearrange
\[\Delta Y_t=\Pi Y_{t-1}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\Gamma_i\Delta Y_{t-i}+d+u_t\]
\[\Pi=\sum_{i=1}^p A_i-I,\qquad \Gamma_i=-\sum_{j=i+1}^p A_j\]
\[\boxed{\Pi=\alpha\beta'}\]

\(\beta\): long-run relation

Defines disequilibrium. Normalization fixes its scale.

\(\alpha\): adjustment

Measures how each variable responds to lagged disequilibrium.

Experiment: who adjusts?

For the simplified system, z = y − x, with no new shocks or lagged differences. Both series start apart.

Estimated WTI model

Ordering: deferred contract first, front month second. An unrestricted constant allows deterministic drift; the rank-one vector is normalized on the deferred contract.

06 · Beyond two variables

How many equilibrium relationships?

With n variables, we can have more than one independent stationary combination. The cointegration rank counts stationary directions, not pairs of variables.

\[r=\operatorname{rank}(\Pi),\qquad \text{Number of common stochastic trends}=n-r\]

Consider three variables. In the examples below, the S terms are independent random walks and u, v and w are stationary disturbances.

Rank 0 · No cointegration

\[X_t=S_{1,t},\quad Y_t=S_{2,t},\quad Z_t=S_{3,t}\]

Three stochastic trends. No stationary linear combination (other than the zero combination).

Rank 1 · One equilibrium

\[X_t=S_{1,t},\quad Y_t=2S_{1,t}+u_t,\quad Z_t=S_{2,t}\]

Two stochastic trends. One stationary combination: \(Y_t-2X_t\).

Rank 2 · Two equilibria

\[X_t=S_t,\quad Y_t=2S_t+u_t,\quad Z_t=3S_t+v_t\]

One stochastic trend. Two independent stationary combinations: \(Y_t-2X_t\) and \(Z_t-3X_t\).

Rank 3 · Full rank

\[X_t=u_t,\quad Y_t=v_t,\quad Z_t=w_t\]

No stochastic trends. All three variables are stationary. This is not a system of cointegrated I(1) variables.

Experiment: change the rank

Cointegration is a property of the system, not necessarily of pairs. For example, if X and Y are independent random walks and Z = X + Y + u, then Z − X − Y is stationary even though no pair is cointegrated.
07 · Model selection

From the Johansen test to the right model

The same sequential testing procedure applies regardless of the number of variables.

Trace test

\[LR_{trace}(r)=-T\sum_{i=r+1}^{n}\log(1-\hat\lambda_i)\]

Null: rank at most r. Alternative: rank greater than r.

Maximum eigenvalue test

\[LR_{max}(r)=-T\log(1-\hat\lambda_{r+1})\]

Null: rank r. Alternative: rank r + 1.

Use the appropriate critical values for the test and deterministic specification. Begin at r = 0, and stop at the first null that is not rejected.

If the trace and maximum eigenvalue tests disagree, or the rank conflicts with the integration-order evidence, revisit lag length, deterministic terms, structural breaks and diagnostics. A failure to reject a unit root is not proof of one. Stationary variables can themselves contribute to the rank; I(2) variables require a different framework.
08 · Empirical workflow

How to conduct a cointegration analysis

A practical checklist for your own empirical applications.

  1. Explore the data

    Align observations and frequency, plot levels and transformations, and motivate possible long-run relationships economically.

  2. Determine the integration order

    Run ADF tests in levels and first differences with suitable deterministic terms and lag lengths. Check that the standard I(1) framework is appropriate.

  3. Choose the specification

    Select the levels VAR order p using AIC, and specify constants or trends (including whether they belong inside or outside the cointegrating relation). The VECM has p − 1 lagged differences.

  4. Determine the cointegration rank

    Apply Johansen sequentially using the selected lag and deterministic specification. Choose a VAR in differences, VECM or VAR in levels accordingly.

  5. Estimate the model

    For a VECM, estimate the cointegrating vectors, adjustment coefficients and short-run dynamics. Normalize the cointegrating vectors.

  6. Interpret the coefficients

    What is the equilibrium? Which variables adjust? In what direction and with what intensity? With several relations, assess the economic interpretation of each normalized vector.

  7. Validate the model

    Check residual serial correlation (e.g. Portmanteau test), stability of the transitory dynamics and robustness to alternative plausible specifications. Revisit earlier steps if diagnostics are unsatisfactory.

  8. Identify the shocks

    Choose an identification strategy if structural interpretation is required. Justify its economic assumptions rather than treating orthogonalization as causal identification.

  9. Analyse impulse responses

    Inspect responses in levels, their confidence intervals and the responses of the cointegrating combinations. Distinguish permanent effects on levels from transitory effects on deviations.

  10. Draw economic conclusions

    Connect the estimated dynamics to economic theory, and distinguish evidence from plausible mechanisms and identification assumptions.

The goal is not simply to find cointegration. It is to choose a suitable model, understand the equilibrium and explain how the system responds to shocks.
